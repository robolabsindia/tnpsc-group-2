# Earth, Universe & Solar System (TNPSC Group II Geography)

## 1. Introduction
```
```
This study material covers the fundamental concepts of the Universe, Earth, and the Solar System as prescribed by the Department of Employment and Training, Government of Tamilnadu, for the TNPSC Group II Exam.

## 2. The Solar System & The Sun
```
```
* **The Sun**: The head of the solar family. It is a large gaseous ball of fire and the only celestial body in the solar system that emits its own light. Its gravity holds the solar system together.
* **Distance**: The Earth is approximately 150 million kms away from the Sun.
* **The Solar System**: Consists of eight planets revolving around the sun in definite elliptical paths called Orbits. All planets revolve in an anti-clockwise direction (from a bird's eye view from the sun's north pole).

## 3. The Planets
```
```
### Mercury
```
```
* **Position & Size**: 1st planet from the sun; smallest planet.
* **Temperature**: Max 350°C (day), Min -170°C (night). 
* **Atmosphere**: Absence of atmosphere makes it unable to hold daytime heat.
* **Key Facts**: Swiftest planet (shortest orbit period). Second densest planet after Earth. No satellites.

### Venus (Shining Planet)
```
```
* **Position & Size**: 2nd planet from the sun; nearest planet to Earth.
* **Temperature**: Hottest planet (Max ~480°C) because 98% of its atmosphere is CO2.
* **Visibility**: Known as the 'Morning Star' (before dawn) and 'Evening Star' (after sunset).
* **Key Facts**: Rotates from east to west. Earth's twin (similar radius, mass, density). No satellites. Slowest rotating planet.

### Earth (Living Planet)
```
```
* **Position**: 3rd planet from the sun.
* **Composition**: 71% water, 29% land.
* **Geography**: 7 Continents (Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Europe, Antarctica, Australia) and 4 Oceans (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic).
* **Age**: Estimated at 4.6 × 10⁹ years via radioactive dating.
* **Time & Rotation**:
  * **Mean solar day**: ~24 hrs (noon to noon).
  * **Sidereal day**: 23 hrs, 56 min, 4 seconds (360° rotation relative to a star).
* **Seasons**: Caused by Earth's revolution and the changing direction of its tilt.
* **Satellite**: Moon.

### Mars (Red Planet)
```
```
* **Temperature**: 23°C (day), -101°C (night).
* **Landscape**: Dry rivers, extinct volcanoes, stormy deserts, snow-covered poles. Lacks greenery.
* **Color**: Appears red due to high amounts of iron oxides (ferric oxides) and dusty layers created by 270 km/hr stormy winds.
* **Satellites**: Deimos, Phobos.

### Jupiter
```
```
* **Size**: Biggest planet (1/10th the size of the sun).
* **Features**: Great 'Redspot' (tremendous storm sending strong radiowaves). Upper clouds contain poisonous ammonia & methane.
* **Axis**: Almost perpendicular (2° tilt), meaning it has no seasons.
* **Key Facts**: Fastest rotating planet. Highest number of satellites.
* **Major Satellites**: Ganymede (largest in solar system), Io, Europa, Callisto.

### Saturn
```
```
* **Position**: 6th planet. Second largest planet.
* **Features**: 7 elegant, multi-coloured rings. Cyclonic winds of 1500 km/hr.
* **Density**: Least dense planet (Specific gravity 0.7). It would float if placed in a large enough ocean.
* **Satellites**: Titan, Tethys.

### Uranus
```
```
* **Position**: 7th planet.
* **Axis**: Tilted about 98°. It "rolls on its sides" while revolving.
* **Rotation**: Rotates from east to west.
* **Satellites**: Miranda, Ariel, Titania, Oberon.

### Neptune
```
```
* **Position**: 8th planet.
* **Features**: Very cold, whipped by supersonic winds. Takes 165 earth years to orbit the sun. Has not completed a single orbit since its discovery in 1846.
* **Color**: Appears blue due to methane gas.
* **Satellites**: Triton.

## 4. Dwarf Planets
```
```
* Grouped in 2006. They revolve around the sun but lack the "gravitation muscle" to sweep up or scatter objects near their orbits.
* **Examples**: Pluto, Eris, Ceres, Charon.
* **Note**: Pluto is the slowest revolving planet/dwarf planet.

## 5. Classification & Visibility
```
```
* **Solid Planets**: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. (Formed close to the sun; solar winds blew away gaseous contents).
* **Gaseous Planets**: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
* **Naked Eye Visibility**: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn.
* **Telescope Required**: Uranus, Neptune.

## 6. Stars and the Universe
```
```
* **Stars**: The Sun is the closest star. Alpha Centauri is the closest after the Sun. Stars twinkle; planets glow without flickering.
* **Galaxy**: Millions of constellations of stars.
* **Universe**: Thousands of galaxies. Vast and ever-expanding.
* **Akash Ganga**: The white streak of light (Milky Way) seen on a clear night.

## 7. Eclipses
```
```
* **Lunar Eclipse**: Occurs on a Full Moon day when Earth comes between the Moon and Sun. Lasts up to 3 hrs 40 mins (totality up to 1 hr 40 mins). Does not occur every full moon because the Moon's orbit is inclined at 5° to the ecliptic.
* **Solar Eclipse**: Occurs on a New Moon day when the Moon comes between the Sun and Earth. Totality rarely lasts over 7 minutes (never more than 8 mins). The Moon's orbit is inclined at 5° 9' to the ecliptic.

## 8. Asteroids, Meteors, and Comets
```
```
* **Asteroids**: Clusters of tiny stones and big rocks (300-400 km diameter) found between Mars and Jupiter. *Tamil named asteroids: VynuPappu, Sarabai, Ramanuja.*
* **Meteoroids/Meteors**: 
  * *[Source Note: The document defines particles burnt by atmospheric friction as "meteorites" and those that withstand friction and reach the surface as "meteors". This contradicts standard scientific terminology but is preserved here for source fidelity.]*
* **Comets**: Rocks made of dust and ice with a long reflective tail that appears when ice melts near the Sun.

## 9. The Moon
```
```
* **Nature**: Earth's only satellite. Does not revolve around the Sun. Measures 1/4th of Earth's diameter.
* **Distance & Time**: ~3,84,401 kms from Earth. Takes 27.3 days to revolve and rotate (keeping the same face towards Earth).
* **Surface**: No atmosphere. Water available only as moisture. Features mountains, plateaus, valleys, and craters (volcanic or meteorite impacts).
