## 1. Soil Formation & Profile
* **Definition**: Soil is a mixture of rock debris and organic materials that develop on the earth's surface.
* **Factors of Formation**: Relief, parent material, climate, vegetation, other life-forms, time, and human activities.
* **Components**: Mineral particles, humus, water, and air.
* **Soil Profile (Horizons)**:
  * **Horizon A**: Topmost zone. Contains organic materials incorporated with mineral matter, nutrients, and water (essential for plant growth).
  * **Horizon B**: Transition zone between A and C. Contains matter derived from both above and below. Mineral matter is noticeably weathered.
  * **Horizon C**: Composed of loose parent material. The first stage in the soil formation process.
  * **Bedrock**: The solid rock underneath Horizon C.

## 3. Major Soil Types in India
### Alluvial Soils
* **Occurrence**: Widespread in northern plains and river valleys (covers ~40% of India's total area). Found in deltas of the east coast.
* **Types**: 
  * **Khadar**: New alluvium, deposited annually by floods, rich in fine silts.
  * **Bhangar**: Older alluvium, deposited away from flood plains.
  * *Note*: Both contain calcareous concretions known as **Kankars**.
* **Characteristics**: Depositional soils transported by rivers. Rich in **potash**, poor in **phosphorous**. Color varies from light grey to ash grey.

### Black Soil (Regur / Black Cotton Soil)
* **Occurrence**: Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, AP, parts of TN). Very deep in upper Godavari and Krishna reaches.
* **Characteristics**: Clayey, deep, and impermeable. Swells and becomes sticky when wet; develops wide cracks when dry, leading to **'self-ploughing'**. Retains moisture for a long time.
* **Chemical Properties**: Rich in lime, iron, magnesia, alumina, and potash. **Deficient in phosphorous, nitrogen, and organic matter**.

### Red and Yellow Soil
* **Occurrence**: Eastern and Southern Deccan Plateau, piedmont zone of Western Ghats, parts of Orissa, Chhattisgarh.
* **Characteristics**: Develops on crystalline igneous rocks in low rainfall areas. **Red color** is due to wide diffusion of **iron**. Looks yellow when in a hydrated form. Generally poor in nitrogen, phosphorous, and humus.

### Laterite Soil
* **Origin**: Derived from the Latin word 'Later' meaning **brick**.
* **Occurrence**: Higher areas of the Peninsular plateau (Karnataka, Kerala, TN, MP, hilly Orissa, Assam).
* **Characteristics**: Develops in high temperature and high rainfall areas due to **intense leaching** (lime and silica are leached away). Rich in iron oxide and aluminium; poor in organic matter, N, phosphate, and calcium.
* **Uses**: Red laterite soils in TN, AP, and Kerala are suitable for **cashewnut**. Widely cut as bricks for house construction.

### Arid & Saline Soils
* **Arid Soils**: Found in western Rajasthan. Sandy, saline, and lack moisture/humus. Lower horizons have a **'Kankar' layer** (calcium) that restricts water infiltration.
* **Saline Soils**: Found in western Gujarat, eastern coast deltas, Sunderbans, and Rann of Kuchchh. Contain excess sodium, potassium, and magnesium. 
  * *Green Revolution Impact*: Excessive irrigation in Punjab/Haryana causes capillary action, depositing salt on the top layer. **Gypsum** is added to solve this.

### Peaty & Forest Soils
* **Peaty Soils**: Found in heavy rainfall areas (Northern Bihar, southern Uttaranchal, coastal WB, Orissa, TN). Rich in organic matter/humus (**40-50%**). Heavy and black.
* **Forest Soils**: Found in forested mountain areas. Loamy/silty in valley sides, coarse-grained in upper slopes. Acidic with low humus in snow-bound Himalayas.
