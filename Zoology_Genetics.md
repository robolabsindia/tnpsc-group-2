# Genetics (TNPSC Group II Zoology)

## 1. Historical Background
* **Genetics**: Branch of science that deals with heredity (transmission of characters from parents to offspring).
* **Father of Genetics**: G.J. Mendel
  * Born: 1822, Czechoslovakia (Heinzendorf, Silesia)
  * Worked as a teacher in Imperial Royal School
  * Research conducted in Brunn for 9 years (1856-1865)
  * Book: *Experiments on Plant Hybridization*
* **Rediscovery of Mendel's Work (1900)**:
  * Hugo de Vries (Holland) – worked on *Oenothera lamarckiana*
  * Carl Correns (Germany) – worked on Xenia, peas, maize
  * Erich Von Tschermak (Austria) – worked on flowering plants

## 2. Mendel's Experiments
* **Plant Used**: *Pisum sativum* (Garden Peas)
* **Reasons for Choosing Pisum sativum**:
  * Self-pollinating flowers of peculiar structure
  * Short growth and short life cycle
  * Easy for artificial cross-pollination
  * Had contrasting heritable characters
  * Various available varieties
  * Out of 34 characters, he researched on 7 characters

### Seven Characters Studied
| Character | Dominant | Recessive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Length | Long | Short |
| Flower Position | Axial | Terminal |
| Pod Shape | Inflated | Constricted |
| Pod Colour | Green | Yellow |
| Seed Shape | Round | Wrinkled |
| Seed Coat Colour | Grey | White |
| Colour of Cotyledon | Yellow | Green |

### Generations & Crosses
* **F1 (First Filial Generation)**: Resultant hybrids of parent generation by cross-fertilization.
* **F2 (Second Filial Generation)**: F1 progeny allowed to self-fertilize among themselves.

### Ratios
| Cross Type | Ratio |
| :--- | :--- |
| Monohybrid Cross – Phenotypic Ratio | 3:1 |
| Monohybrid Cross – Genotypic Ratio | 1:2:1 |
| Monohybrid Test Cross Ratio | 1:1 |
| Dihybrid Cross – Phenotypic Ratio | 9:3:3:1 |
| Dihybrid Test Cross Ratio | 1:1:1:1 |

* **Punnett Square**: Made by Punnett to prove Mendel's results. Also called **Chequered Square**.

## 3. Mendel's Laws
### From Monohybrid Cross:
1. **Law of Dominance**: In F1, only the dominant character is expressed; the recessive is suppressed.
2. **Law of Segregation** (Law of Purity of Gametes): Alleles separate during gamete formation.

### From Dihybrid Cross:
3. **Law of Independent Assortment** (Law of Random Assortment): Characters are inherited independently.

## 4. Jumping Genes
* **Discovered by**: Barbara McClintock
* **Organism**: Maize
* **Discovery**: Presence of movable genetic elements which could detach from one site and move to new positions in either the same or different chromosomes.
* **Also called**: Transposable elements, transposons, insertion elements, or jumping genes.

## 5. Glossary of Genetics Terms
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gene** | Factors controlling a single character |
| **Phenotype** | Expression of a character |
| **Genotype** | The genes controlling a character |
| **Allele** | Each of two or more alternative forms of a gene (T, t) |
| **Allelomorph** | Character having different phenotype |
| **Homozygous** | Having identical alleles at corresponding chromosomal loci (TT, tt) |
| **Heterozygous** | Having dissimilar alleles at corresponding chromosomal loci (Tt) |
| **Dominant** | Expressed character in F1 |
| **Recessive** | Suppressed character in F1 |
| **Hybrid** | A composite of mixed origin |
| **Emasculation** | Neutering a male animal by removing the testicles |
| **Back Cross** | Mate a hybrid of F1 generation with one of its parents |
| **Test Cross** | Cross between an organism with unknown genotype and a homozygous recessive organism |

## 6. Gene Therapy
* **Definition**: Replacement of corrective genes in place of defective genes in humans.
* **Types**:
  1. Somatic cell gene therapy
  2. Germ line cell gene therapy
* Both may be employed for treating inherited diseases.

## 7. Human Chromosomes
* **Male**: XY
* **Female**: XX
* **Total**: 23 pairs
* Pedigree analysis is used to study inheritance patterns.

## 8. Genetic Engineering
* **Definition**: Technology of preparing recombinant DNA in vitro by cutting up DNA molecules and splicing together fragments from more than one organism.
* **Basic Principle**: Gene transfer, achieved by various methods to produce recombinant proteins, genetically modified microorganisms, transgenic plants, and transgenic animals.
* **Key Features**:
  * Production of beneficial proteins and enzymes in surplus quantities
  * Creation of transgenic plants, animals, and genetically modified microorganisms with new characters

### Restriction Enzymes
* Also called **Restriction Endonucleases** or **Genetic Scissors**
* Ability to cut molecules of DNA
* Recognize a unique sequence of nucleotides (usually 4-6 base pairs in length)
* Complementary DNA strand has the same sequence but in reverse direction

### Uses of Biotechnology
1. Manufacture of liquors
2. Manufacture of enzymes
3. Manufacture of antibiotics
4. Manufacture of acetic acid
5. Manufacture of vitamins
6. Manufacture of vaccines
7. Manufacture of steroids
8. Manufacture of monoclonal antibodies

## 10. Types of Cloning
| Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Molecular Cloning** | Isolation of a defined DNA sequence (gene) and obtaining multiple copies within a living organism. Used in large-scale protein production. |
| **Embryo Cloning** | Duplicates the process nature uses to produce twins/triplets. Cells removed from fertilized embryo and encouraged to develop into duplicate embryos. |
| **Reproductive Cloning** | Producing a duplicate of an existing animal. Most famous: **Dolly the Sheep**. DNA from embryo replaced with DNA from adult animal. |
| **Therapeutic Cloning** | Stem cells removed from embryo to produce tissue or organ for transplant back into the person who supplied the DNA. Embryo dies in the process. |

## 11. Bio-Sensors & Bio-Chips
### Bio-Sensors
* **Definition**: A device which uses a living organism or biological molecules (enzymes or antibodies) to detect the presence of chemicals.
* **Components**: Biological element + Physicochemical detector (transducer) + Reader device.

### Bio-Chips
* **Definition**: A microchip designed to function in a biological environment, especially inside a living organism.
* **Uses**: Multi-purpose tracking device, Medical/Scientific device, Identification system, Military/Defence mechanism, Business.

## 12. Stem Cells
* **Definition**: Undifferentiated biological cells that can differentiate into specialized cells and can divide (through mitosis) to produce more stem cells.
* **Types in Mammals**:
  * Embryonic stem cells (from inner cell mass of blastocysts)
  * Adult stem cells (found in various tissues)
* **Sources of Adult Stem Cells**: Bone marrow, Adipose tissue, Blood (apheresis), Umbilical cord blood.
* **Stem Cell Potency**:
  * **Totipotent (Omnipotent)**: Can give rise to any of the 220 cell types + extra-embryonic cells (placenta).
  * **Pluripotent**: Can give rise to all cell types of the body (but NOT the placenta).
  * **Multipotent**: Can develop into a limited number of cell types in a particular lineage.
