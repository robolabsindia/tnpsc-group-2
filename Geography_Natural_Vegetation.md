## 1. Introduction to Natural Vegetation
* **Definition**: A plant community that has been left undisturbed over a long time, allowing its individual species to adjust to climate and soil conditions fully.
* **Regional Variety in India**: 
  * *Himalayan heights*: Temperate vegetation.
  * *Western Ghats & Andaman/Nicobar*: Tropical rain forests.
  * *Deltaic regions*: Tropical forests and mangroves.
  * *Rajasthan (Desert/Semi-desert)*: Cacti, bushes, and thorny vegetation.

## 2. Types of Forests
### Tropical Evergreen & Semi-Evergreen Forests
* **Tropical Evergreen**: 
  * *Occurrence*: Western slopes of Western Ghats, NE region, A&N Islands.
  * *Conditions*: Rainfall > 200 cm, Mean annual temp > 22°C.
  * *Characteristics*: Well-stratified, trees reach up to 60m. No definite time for shedding leaves.
  * *Trees*: Rosewood, mahogany, aini, ebony.
* **Semi-Evergreen**: Found in less rainy parts of the above regions. Mixture of evergreen and moist deciduous trees. *Trees*: White cedar, hollock, kail.

### Tropical Deciduous Forests (Monsoon Forests)
* **Moist Deciduous**: 
  * *Occurrence*: NE states, eastern slopes of Western Ghats, Odisha.
  * *Conditions*: Rainfall 100-200 cm. Trees remain leafless during the dry season.
  * *Trees*: Teak, sal, shisham, hurra, mahua, amla, semul, kusum, sandalwood.
* **Dry Deciduous**: 
  * *Occurrence*: Rainier areas of the Peninsula, plains of UP and Bihar.
  * *Conditions*: Rainfall 70-100 cm. Parkland landscape with open stretches. Trees shed leaves completely in the dry season.
  * *Trees*: Tendu, palas, amaltas, bel, khair, axlewood.

### Tropical Thorn Forests
* **Occurrence**: Semi-arid areas of SW Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, UP.
* **Conditions**: Rainfall < 50 cm.
* **Characteristics**: Scrub vegetation, grasses, and shrubs. Plants remain leafless for most of the year.
* **Trees**: Babool, ber, wild date palm, khair, neem, khejri, palas.

### Montane Forests
* **Northern Mountain Forests (Himalayas)**: Vegetation changes with altitude.
  * *Foothills*: Deciduous forests.
  * *1,000 - 2,000m*: Wet temperate forests (Oak, chestnut).
  * *1,500 - 1,750m*: Pine forests (Chir pine - highly commercial). Deodar (endemic to western Himalayas).
  * *3,000 - 4,000m*: Alpine forests and pastures (Silver firs, junipers, birch, rhododendrons). Used for **transhumance** by tribes like Gujjars, Bakarwals, Bhotiyas, and Gaddis.
  * *Higher altitudes*: Tundra (mosses and lichens).
* **Southern Mountain Forests**: Found in Western Ghats, Vindhyas, Nilgiris. Temperate forests in higher regions are called **Sholas** (Nilgiris, Anaimalai, Palani hills). *Trees*: Magnolia, laurel, cinchona, wattle.

### Littoral and Swamp Forests
* **Mangroves**: Grow along coasts in salt marshes, tidal creeks, and estuaries. Consist of salt-tolerant species.
* **Area**: Spread over **6,740 sq. km** (7% of the world’s mangrove forests).
* **Key Locations**: Highly developed in A&N Islands and **Sunderbans (West Bengal)**. Also found in Mahanadi, Godavari, and Krishna deltas.

## 3. Forest and Wildlife Conservation
### Key Terminology
* **National Parks**: Reserved forests preserving natural vegetation and wildlife in their natural environment.
* **Wildlife Sanctuaries**: Forest portions where hunting and poaching are strictly prohibited.
* **Endangered Species**: Species in danger of extinction (e.g., Asiatic elephant).
* **Endemic Species**: Species found only in specific localized areas (e.g., Andaman wild pig).
* **Bio-diversity**: The existence of millions of living beings simultaneously, integrated through multiple networks of interdependencies.

### Flora, Fauna & Legislation
* **Biodiversity Stats**: India has ~8% of the world's total species. **81,000 species of fauna** and **47,000 species of flora** (15,000 flowering plants are endemic).
* **Indian Wildlife Protection Act (1972)**: Banned hunting, provided legal habitat protection, and restricted wildlife trade.
* **Notifications (1980 & 1986)**: Added insects (butterflies, moths, beetles, dragonflies) to the protected list.
* **Wildlife Act (1990)**: Added plants to the protected list for the first time.
* **Project Tiger (1973)**: Launched to protect tiger populations. Major reserves include Corbett (UK), Sundarbans (WB), Bandhavgarh & Kanha (MP), Manas (Assam), and Periyar (Kerala).

## 4. Forest Resources & Distribution
### Forest Classifications
* **Reserved Forests**: Most valuable for conservation. Make up **54.4%** of total forest land.
* **Protected Forests**: Protected from further depletion. Make up **29.2%**.
  * *(Note: Reserved + Protected = Permanent Forests)*
* **Unclassed Forests**: Other forests and wastelands (govt/private). Make up **16.4%**.

### Distribution Highlights
* **Largest Permanent Forest Area**: Madhya Pradesh.
* **Dense Forests**: North-Eastern states, A&N Islands, Uttarakhand.
* **Unclassed Forests**: North-Eastern states and Gujarat.

## 5. Community Involvement & Multi-purpose Projects
* **Chipko Andolan**: Himalayas; successfully resisted deforestation and encouraged afforestation.
* **Beej Bachao Andolan**: Tehri and Navdanya; proved adequate diversified crop production without synthetic chemicals.
* **Joint Forest Management (JFM)**: Involves local communities in the management and restoration of degraded forests.
* **Multi-purpose Projects**: Dams built for power, water supply, flood control, and navigation. Proclaimed as the **'temples of modern India'**. Examples: Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud.
