## 1. Introduction to Endocrinology
* **Endocrinology**: Study of endocrine glands and their secretions.
* **Father of Endocrine Glands**: T. Addision (Thomas Addison).
* **Hormones**: Chemical messengers secreted directly into the blood (ductless glands).
  * *Term coined by*: E.H. Starling (1906).
  * *Earliest known hormone*: Insulin.
  * *Insulin extracted by*: Banting & Macbet (Macleod) in 1923.
  * *Thyroxine identified by*: E.C. Kondall (Kendall) in 1914.
  * *Molecular structure of Insulin*: Sanger (1954) - awarded Nobel Prize.
  * *Pituitary functions described by*: Fernando Housie (Houssay) in 1947.
* **Chemical Nature of Hormones**:
  * *Amino acid*: Thyroxine, adrenaline, noradrenaline.
  * *Amine/Catecholamine*: Epinephrine, norepinephrine.
  * *Steroid*: Adrenal cortex hormones.
  * *Proteins/Polypeptides*: Oxytocin, vasopressin, relaxin, insulin.
  * *Glycoprotein*: FSH, LH (CH), TSH.
  * *Fats*: Prostaglandin.
* **General Characteristics**: Produced in low amounts, easily diffusible, water-soluble, low molecular weight, destroyed after use, not stored in the body, non-antigenic (no antibody formation), regulated by feedback mechanisms.

## 2. Pituitary Gland (Hypophysis)
* **Known as**: Master Gland / Ring Master of the Orchestra.
* **Name given by**: Vesalius.
* **Structure**: Located in the brain. Made of two lobes (Anterior and Posterior) joined by a funnel-like **infundibulum**. Length: 1 cm, Breadth: 1.5 cm, Weight: 0.5 gm.
* **Removal**: Hypophysectomy.

### Anterior Lobe (Adenohypophysis)
1. **STH (Somatotrophic / Growth Hormone)**: Controls overall and bone growth; retains N, K, P, Na.
   * *Hyposecretion*: Dwarfism (children), Simmond’s disease (adults).
   * *Hypersecretion*: Gigantism (children), Acromegaly (adults).
2. **TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone)**: Stimulates thyroid gland and iodine uptake.
3. **ACTH (Adreno Cortico Tropic Hormone)**: Stimulates adrenal cortex, melanocytes, transports fats.
4. **FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone)**: Males (spermatogenesis); Females (ovary growth, graafian follicle development).
5. **LTH (Prolactin)**: Mammary gland growth, milk secretion, acts on corpus luteum.
6. **LH (Luteinizing Hormone)**: Release of ovum, formation of corpus luteum.
7. **ICSH**: Stimulates interstitial cells in testes to secrete testosterone.

### Posterior Lobe (Neurohypophysis)
1. **Oxytocin (Pitocin)**: Stimulates uterine contraction (parturition) and milk ejection.
2. **Vasopressin (ADH / Pitressin)**: Reduces urine output (water balance), controls BP.
   * *Hyposecretion*: **Diabetes insipidus** (Symptoms: Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia).

## 3. Thyroid & Parathyroid Glands
### Thyroid Gland
* **Structure**: Largest endocrine gland, located in the neck, 2 lobes connected by **isthmus**.
* **Key Scientists**: Isolated by Kocher; Crystals by Kendall; Molecular structure by Harrington.
* **Functions**: Nervous system development, controls BMR, reduces cholesterol, stimulates metamorphosis.
* **Hypothyroidism**:
  * *Simple/Endemic Goitre*: Gland enlarges.
  * *Cretinism* (Children): Retarded sex organs, low IQ, deformed bones.
  * *Myxoedema / Gull’s Disease* (Adults): Weight gain, low pulse, puffy/dry skin, feels cold.
* **Hyperthyroidism**: Early sexual maturity (children); **Graves disease** (adults - treated by surgery).

### Parathyroid Gland
* **Structure**: Two pairs embedded in thyroid lobes. Purple, elongated.
* **Key Scientists**: Discovered by Collip; Structure by Sandstrom; Molecular by Pot; Crystals by Craig.
* **Hormones**:
  1. **Parathormone**: Half-life 20-30 mins. Works on bones (structure), kidneys (pH), intestine (Vit D synthesis).
  2. **Calcitonin**: Lowers blood calcium, secretes HCl in stomach. Antagonistic to Parathormone.

## 4. Adrenal Gland (Suprarenal / Emergency Gland)
* **Location**: Top of the kidneys. Discovered by Eustachian.
* **Structure**: Cortex (80%) and Medulla (20%).
* **Cortex Zones**:
  1. *Zona glomerulosa* (Outer) - Mineralocorticoids.
  2. *Zona fasciculata* (Middle) - Glucocorticoids.
  3. *Zona reticularis* (Inner) - Sex Hormones.
* **Medulla Hormones**:
  1. **Adrenaline (Epinephrine) - 80%**: Emergency hormone. Increases blood flow, heart rate, respiration, blood glucose. 
     * *3F*: Fear, Fight, Flight.
     * *4S*: Sugar metabolism, Salt retaining, Sex hormone, Source of energy.
  2. **Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine) - 20%**: Raises BP, antagonistic to adrenaline.

## 5. Gonads (Testis & Ovary)
### Testis
* **Leydig cells**: Produce Androgens (Testosterone).
* **Testosterone**: Male secondary sexual characters (deep voice, beard), genital organ enlargement.

### Ovary
1. **Estrogens**: Secreted by graafian follicle (induced by FSH). Female secondary sexual characters, development of reproductive organs.
2. **Progesterone**: Secreted by corpus luteum. Prepares endometrium, maintains pregnancy (**Pregnancy hormone / Anti-abortion hormone**), suppresses uterine contractions.
3. **Relaxin**: Secreted by corpus luteum during pregnancy. Relaxes pubic symphysis and pelvic girdle for easy childbirth.

## 6. Thymus, Pineal & Pancreas
### Thymus
* Nearest to the heart; partly endocrine, partly lymphoid.
* Produces **thymine**; contains **Hassell’s corpuscles** (Thymic cells).
* Crucial for immunity and production of **T-cells (T-lymphocytes)** and antibodies in infants.

### Pineal Body
* Found in the brain; represents the vestige of the **3rd eye**. Atrophies at age 7.
* Secretes **Melatonin**: Regulates sleep/wakefulness, metabolism, pigmentation, and menstrual cycle.

### Pancreas (Mixed Gland)
* **Islets of Langerhans** (Endocrine portion):
  * **Alpha ($\alpha$) cells**: Produce **Glucagon** (increases blood sugar, stimulates glycogenolysis).
  * **Beta ($\beta$) cells**: Produce **Insulin** (converts glucose to glycogen, lowers blood sugar).
  * **Gamma/Delta ($\gamma$/$\delta$) cells**: Produce Somatostatin.
* **Diabetes Mellitus**: Caused by insulin deficiency. 
  * *Glucose Levels*: Fasting (70-110 mg/dl), Normal (80-120 mg/dl), Excess (140-150 mg/dl), Abnormal (300-400 mg/dl).
  * *Type I*: Genetic, body unable to produce insulin.
  * *Type II*: More common, body produces insulin but unable to use it effectively.
