## 1. Introduction to Disasters
* **Definition**: An unexpected event that kills a lot of people or animals and causes a lot of damage.
* **Etymology**: The term 'DISASTER' is derived from the French word **'DISASTRE'**, meaning **'an evil star'**.
* **Categories**:
  * **Natural Disasters**: Earthquakes, Floods, Cyclones, Droughts, Tsunamis, Volcanic Eruptions, Landslides.
  * **Man-Made Disasters**: Industrial/Chemical accidents, Nuclear accidents, Biological attacks, Road/Rail/Fire accidents.

## 2. Types of Natural Disasters

### Earthquakes
* Caused by sudden release of energy from the Earth's interior.
* **Measurement**: Richter Scale (Magnitude), Mercalli Scale (Intensity/Damage).
* **India's Vulnerability**: Himalayan region (most vulnerable), North-East India, Kutch (Gujarat). Zone 5 = Very High Damage Risk.
* **Precautions**: Earthquake-resistant building design, awareness campaigns, early warning systems.

### Floods
* **Causes**: Heavy rainfall, overflowing rivers, dam bursts, poor drainage.
* **Flood-Prone Areas in India**: Brahmaputra and Ganga basins, coastal regions during cyclones.
* **Mitigation**: Embankments, dams, flood forecasting, afforestation in catchment areas.

### Cyclones
* **Definition**: Violent storms with extremely strong winds that move in a circle.
* **Indian Vulnerability**: Eastern coast (Bay of Bengal) is more cyclone-prone than the Western coast (Arabian Sea).
* **Precautions**: Early warning systems, cyclone shelters, coastal afforestation (mangroves).

### Droughts
* **Definition**: Prolonged period of abnormally low rainfall leading to a shortage of water.
* **Types**:
  * **Meteorological Drought**: When actual rainfall is significantly less than the climatological mean.
  * **Hydrological Drought**: Prolonged deficit leading to depletion of surface and groundwater.
  * **Agricultural Drought**: When soil moisture and rainfall are inadequate to support healthy crop growth.
* **Drought-Prone Areas**: Rajasthan, Gujarat, parts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and Tamil Nadu.
* **Mitigation**:
  * Drought-resisting crops or crops demanding less water should be cultivated.
  * Afforestation to retain humidity and promote precipitation.
  * Ban on cutting of trees and clearing of forests.
  * **Rooftop water harvesting** should be adopted in every house.

### Tsunamis
* **Definition**: Seismic sea waves caused by undersea earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides. Term derived from the Japanese words **'Tsu'** (harbor) and **'nami'** (wave).
* **Characteristics**:
  * *Deep Water*: Very long wavelength, small wave height (barely noticeable by ships).
  * *Shallow Water*: Wavelength decreases, wave height increases drastically (15m+).
* **2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami**: Occurred on **26th December 2004**. More than **3,00,000** lives lost. India joined the **International Tsunami Warning System** after this event.

### Landslides
* **Causes**: Heavy rainfall, deforestation, earthquakes, construction on slopes.
* **Vulnerable Areas**: Himalayan regions, Western Ghats, North-Eastern states.
* **Mitigation**: Afforestation, proper drainage, avoiding construction on steep slopes.

## 3. Man-Made Disasters

### Chemical / Industrial Disasters
* **Bhopal Gas Tragedy**: Occurred on **3rd December 1984**. Methyl Isocyanate (MIC) gas leak from the Union Carbide plant. One of the worst industrial disasters in history.
* **Precautions**: Proper safety protocols, regular inspections, emergency response plans.

### Biological Attacks
* A biological attack may not be known in advance; it may be learnt through an announcement on radio or TV.
* **Precautions**: Move away from the site immediately. Cover mouth and nose with cloth that permits easy breathing. Inform public authorities immediately.

### Fire Accidents
* **Precautions**: Inform the Police Station or Fire Station immediately. Learn Dos and Don'ts from the Fire Station. Help government agencies in accidental situations.

## 4. Disaster Management Framework

### Phases of Disaster Management
1. **Prevention**: Building codes, land-use planning, public awareness.
2. **Preparedness**: Training, mock drills, stocking emergency supplies, early warning systems.
3. **Response**: Search and rescue, medical assistance, evacuation, relief distribution.
4. **Recovery / Rehabilitation**: Reconstruction, livelihood restoration, psychological support.

### Institutional Framework in India
* **National Level**:
  * **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)**: Headed by the **Prime Minister**.
  * **National Executive Committee (NEC)**: Headed by the **Home Secretary**. SEC (State Executive Committee) includes Secretaries of all relevant Ministries.
  * **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)**: Specialized force for disaster response.
  * **National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM)**: For capacity building and training.
* **State Level**:
  * **State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA)**: Headed by the **Chief Minister**.
  * **State Executive Committee (SEC)**: Headed by the **Chief Secretary**.
* **District Level**:
  * **District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA)**: Headed by the **District Collector/Magistrate** and co-chaired by the elected representative of the local authority.

### Disaster Management Act, 2005
* Provides for the effective management of disasters and matters connected therewith.
* Established the institutional framework at National, State, and District levels.

### National Policy on Disaster Management (2009)
Key objectives include:
1. Promoting a culture of prevention, preparedness, and resilience.
2. Encouraging mitigation measures based on technology, traditional wisdom, and environmental sustainability.
3. Mainstreaming disaster management into the developmental planning process.
4. Establishing institutional and techno-legal frameworks.
5. Ensuring efficient response mechanisms.
6. Undertaking recovery, reconstruction, and rehabilitation.
7. Ensuring a constructive partnership with media.
8. Ensuring safer living.
9. Promoting a productive and proactive partnership with the media for disaster management.

### International Organizations
* **International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC / "The Federation")**: Deploys assessment teams like **Field Assessment and Coordination Team (FACT)** to affected countries if requested.
* **Emergency Response Units (ERUs)**: Deployed after assessment for specialized response.

## 5. Awareness & Preparedness
* **Awareness**: Making people aware of vulnerability, hazard, and extent of risk.
* **Methods**: Information, Education, and Communication (IEC).
* **Benefits**:
  * Ensures unity, cooperation, and participation.
  * People remain ready to cooperate with helping agencies.
  * Enables protection of lives and properties.
