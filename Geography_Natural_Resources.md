## 1. Introduction & Classification of Resources
Resources are a function of human activities. Human beings transform materials available in the environment into resources. 
* **On the Basis of Origin**:
  * **Biotic Resources**: Obtained from the biosphere and have life (e.g., human beings, flora and fauna, fisheries, livestock).
  * **Abiotic Resources**: Composed of non-living things (e.g., rocks and metals).
* **On the Basis of Exhaustibility**:
  * **Renewable Resources**: Can be renewed or reproduced by physical, chemical, or mechanical processes (e.g., solar and wind energy, water, forests, wildlife). They can be further divided into continuous or flow resources.
  * **Non-Renewable Resources**: Occur over a very long geological time. Minerals and fossil fuels take millions of years to form. Some metals are recyclable, but fossil fuels get exhausted with use.
* **On the Basis of Ownership**:
  * **Individual Resources**: Owned privately by individuals (e.g., plots, houses, ponds, pasture lands).
  * **Community Owned Resources**: Accessible to all members of the community (e.g., village commons, grazing grounds, burial grounds, public parks, picnic spots).
  * **National and International Resources**: Governed by national or international jurisdictions.
* **On the Basis of Status of Development**: Potential, Developed, Stock, and Reserves.

## 2. Natural Resources in India
India is endowed with fertile soil, forests, minerals, and water, but they are unevenly distributed.
* **Soil Resource**: 
  * *Alluvial Soil*: Found in the Northern Great Plains (Sutlej-Ganga) and Brahmaputra Valley. Crops grown include wheat, rice, maize, sugarcane, jute, cotton, rapeseed, mustard, sesame, and linseed.
  * *Black Soil*: Found in Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat. Major crops are cotton and sugarcane.
  * *Degradation*: Improper use destroys soil composition and texture. **Unscientific irrigation causes capillary action**, making the soil alkaline and unsuitable for cultivation.
* **Mineral Resource**: India is rich in iron, coal, mineral oil, manganese, bauxite, chromite, copper, tungsten, gypsum, limestone, and mica. Organizations like the **Geological Survey of India (GSI)** and **Indian Bureau of Mines (IBM)** are engaged in exploration and development.
* **Livestock Resource**: Hills, mountains, and less fertile lands are used for pasture. India maintains a rich domestic animal diversity (goat, sheep, poultry, cattle, buffalo), playing a vital role in improving the socio-economic status of rural masses.

## 3. Resource Management Strategies
* **Reduce**: Using less (e.g., saving electricity by switching off lights, saving water by repairing leaky taps, not wasting food).
* **Recycle**: Collecting plastic, paper, glass, and metal items to manufacture new things instead of extracting fresh raw materials. Requires proper segregation of waste.
* **Reuse**: Using things again and again. This is better than recycling because recycling consumes energy (e.g., reusing envelopes or using plastic jam bottles for kitchen storage).

## 4. Horticulture, Fisheries, and Forests
* **Horticulture**: Diverse agro-climatic conditions facilitate the cultivation of vegetables, fruits, flowers, medicinal/aromatic plants, mushrooms, and plantation crops (tea, coffee, rubber, spices).
* **Fisheries**: Fish production shows an increasing trend. India is one of the leading fish producers globally.
* **Forest Resource**:
  * Known as **‘Green Gold’**. They are renewable resources.
  * Act as a natural 'sponge', regulate climate, and absorb CO2 (the poison of urbanization and industrialization).
  * Prevent soil erosion, increase soil fertility, and provide medicinal herbs, animal habitats, cattle fodder, and domestic fuel.
  * **Conservation**: The **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** was enacted to ensure no forest is diverted for non-forest purposes.
  * **Forest Research Institute (FRI)**: Established at **Dehradun** for forestry research and development.
  * **World Forestry Day**: Observed every **March 21** by planting trees.
